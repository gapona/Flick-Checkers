# Art Sources

Provenance registry for every image that ships under `public/assets/`. Required by CLAUDE.md
"Build Guards & Asset Policy" and enforced by `scripts/check-bundle.mjs`: no image may be
added without a row here. Only **CC0**, **self-generated**, or art the project can show it
owns outright is allowed — an unresolved copyright claim on a sprite or a background is one
of the most common rejection reasons on Playables, exactly as it is for a sound or a font.

Lives at the repo root, not under `public/assets/`, deliberately: it's an internal process
document, not a game asset — `public/` is copied into `dist/` verbatim by Vite, so anything
under it ships.

## Why this file exists at all, and why it did not before

Until the backgrounds below were replaced, every pixel in this repo was arithmetic:
`scripts/make-atlas.mjs` computes the atlas and the backgrounds from drawing commands and a
seeded PRNG, which makes provenance *a property of the repository* rather than a promise in
a registry row — there is no file to trace, only code to read. That is still true of the
atlas, and it is the reason CLAUDE.md says to prefer extending a generator over adding a
file.

The backgrounds are the first deliberate exception. A blurred atmospheric plate is the one
asset class where a generator is genuinely worse than a model, and it is also the safest
class to make an exception for: it is decoration behind the playfield, it carries no game
state, and nothing reads it. Everything a player actually looks at — discs, board, rim,
hazards, HUD — is still computed.

`bg/menu.webp` is the same class: a plate, behind a menu instead of behind a board.
`mascot.webp` is the second exception and a slightly wider one — it is a character rather
than a wash, and a player does look at it. It is still decoration: nothing reads it, no rule
depends on it, and removing it would change no behaviour. The line this project will not
cross is the one it has already refused twice — the **discs, the board and the emblems stay
computed**, because those are what the game is played on and they are recoloured per skin at
runtime, which a baked sprite cannot do.

**The mascot took two runs, and the reason is worth keeping.** The first render put two
stacked moustaches on the face and gave it solid oval eyes with no catchlight; both read as
spectacles. Patching them out afterwards left visible smudges — inpainting has to invent
pixels, and on a shaded face it shows. The fix that worked was a re-render with the defect
made MEASURABLE first: `chapaev_mascot_checks.py` now rejects the old sprite on the shape of
its moustache (a row-profile waist of 0.442 against the control's 0.000) and on a missing eye
catchlight, and its self-test carries five negative controls. Two things that measurement
refuted, both of which had been asserted from looking: the old eyes were not oversized — they
are *smaller* than the control's, by both area and diameter — and a bare COUNT of face
features does not catch the doubled moustache at all, because the two shapes touch and label
as one component.

Two prompt lessons paid for in GPU time and kept in that script: **"no glasses or spectacles"
rendered a character wearing sunglasses**, the textbook CLIP-negation failure, so exclusions
go in as positive statements about what the face contains; and cutting the phrase
`"a cartoon face with"` to buy tokens turned all eight variants into animals with bodies —
that clause is not description, it is what binds the face to the disc.

**The title is NOT here, and that is deliberate**: `gameTitle` is localised (`en`/`es`) and a
baked wordmark would be an English-only wordmark. The logo is typography over a shipped font
— see `src/ui/titleLockup.ts`.

**A diffusion render is not automatically clean.** The rows below record the model, the
prompt provenance and the licence position, because "generated by AI" is a description of a
process, not a licence.

The seeds are recorded so each plate is reproducible from the script alone — provenance that
survives `Remotion/generated/` being cleaned, which is the closest a render gets to the
"read the code" guarantee the atlas has.

**Two gates were added with the last three plates, and both caught something.** The set had grown
to the point where a new plate could be a copy of an old one, and nothing measured that: a plate was
judged against its own brief and never against the catalogue. `MIN_PLATE_DISTANCE` compares the mean
CIELAB of a candidate against every shipped plate and rejects at 12.0 — the same floor
`verify:contrast` uses between two disc sets, and a ratchet rather than a threshold fitted to the
current minimum (the tightest shipped pair, `bg-sand`/`bg-sunset`, sits at 10.7 and is not being
moved). It rejected four attempts across two skins.

The second is a correction to the band guard rather than a new idea. That guard averages each band
across the full 1280px width; the player never sees that width, because `createSceneBackground`
cover-fits a 16:9 plate into a 390x844 phone and keeps the **central 26% of the columns**. So a
plate can be quiet on the guard's number and bright in the strip that actually reaches the screen —
`bg-plum`'s first attempt measured 44.2 to the guard, which left it alone at strength 0, and 66.2 in
the visible column, with the brightest patch of all ten plates sitting exactly where the gear button
goes. The gate applies the guard's OWN targets to the portrait crop, on BOTH bands: gating only the
top was tried first, and the seed that replaced the rejected one came back calm on top and 48.4 on
the bottom, which is where the status line and the two consumable buttons are. The composition had
moved rather than calmed.

**The seven plates that predate both gates are not being re-posted for them.** `bg-frost` and
`bg-emerald` miss the portrait top target (82.9 and 89.1 against 50.6) and six of the seven miss the
bottom one. They were accepted without the measurement, and a gate can only honestly be a ratchet on
what is added after it. It is reachable rather than aspirational: all three new plates pass both
bands, and every one of them sits inside the shipped population under every HUD element measured.

**The portraits are the widest exception in this file, and the line is still where it was.** The
backgrounds are washes and the mascot is decoration; a portrait is a character a player chooses by,
looks at while playing, and is meant to remember. So it is worth being exact about what has and has
not moved: nothing READS a portrait. It carries no game state, no rule depends on it, `Opponent.id`
is what the save stores, and a build with the atlas missing degrades to a drawn stand-in
(`src/ui/portrait.ts`) rather than breaking. The discs, the board, the rim, the hazards and the
emblems are still computed, for the reason they always were — they are recoloured per skin at
runtime, which a baked sprite cannot do.

**Each file is a render plus a deterministic post pass**, not a raw sample out of the model: blur,
vignette, centre light, luma/chroma normalisation, and a **band guard** that darkens the top and
bottom 22% onto a fixed target (top 50.6, bottom 27.0) with a smoothstep ramp. The guard exists
because the pipeline's inherited `centerDetail` rule protects the middle of the plate — which the
board covers — while a portrait phone actually shows the bands, and the bands are where this game's
HUD sits. `bg-emerald` put a light shaft directly behind the gear button before it was added. The
pre-guard masters are kept beside the deliveries (`backgrounds/masters/`), and the strengths are
solved arithmetically rather than dialled by eye, so re-posting is reproducible and costs no GPU:

```
py -3.11 src/scripts/gen_chapaev_skins.py repost
```

The procedural set these replaced is not lost: `scripts/make-atlas.mjs` still builds a complete
one, behind `npm run assets -- --backgrounds`. That flag exists **because** these files are here —
`npm run assets` is a command anyone would run just to refresh the atlas, and it used to overwrite
all four backgrounds without saying so.

## Rules for adding a row

- **Say what made the file.** A generator gets its script path; a model gets its name, the
  weights' licence, and where the prompt came from. "AI-generated" alone is not a row.
- **Stable Diffusion XL** (`stabilityai/stable-diffusion-xl-base-1.0`) is released under the
  CreativeML Open RAIL++-M licence, which permits commercial use of the outputs. Under
  current US Copyright Office guidance a raw model output is not itself copyrightable, which
  means the project cannot register a claim on it — but nor can anyone else, and that is the
  position this game needs.
- **Never register a row for a file you have not looked at.** The check is deliberately dumb:
  it only asks that the filename appears in this file. It cannot tell a truthful row from a
  careless one — that is the author's job, and the whole point of the gate is to make adding
  a file without thinking about its licence impossible to do *silently*.
